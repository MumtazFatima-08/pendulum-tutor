Round 1 Prototype — Physics, Taught by a Machine That Can't Lie

Two pendulums. Three ways to be wrong about them.

A double pendulum is the textbook example of chaos: nudge the start by a hair, and the paths tear apart within seconds. This demo puts a real physics simulator next to two neural predictors — one that learned only from motion data, and one that was also taught the law of energy conservation. Watch which one a student should trust.

What to watch: all three start from the same angle, plus a tiny nudge you control. The green pendulum is the real physics — treat it as truth. The red one only ever saw recorded trajectories; once it's extrapolating past what it memorized, it starts inventing motion that breaks physics (arm length drifts, energy appears from nowhere). The blue one was trained with an extra penalty for violating the pendulum's equations of motion — even when it's uncertain, it stays inside the bounds of what's physically possible.

The lesson underneath the demo: AI that "does the teaching" is only trustworthy if it's honest about the limits of what it knows. This is the same failure mode as an AI tutor confidently explaining a wrong derivation — it sounds fluent, but it's violating a rule it was never forced to respect. Physics-informed training is one concrete way to force that respect, and a student can see it happen here, instead of being told about it in the abstract.
